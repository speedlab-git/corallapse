In one sentence

CoralLapse turns repeat underwater video of coral colonies into a 4D model (3D + time) that measures how much each colony grew or shrank, and writes a monitoring report in which every number is checked against the measurements.

01 / Background

Growth is one of the most useful facts in reef monitoring, and one of the slowest to measure.

Surface area and volume describe a colony better than a single length. Photogrammetry can measure whole-colony change at the millimeter scale, but each survey needs careful photo capture, hours of processing, and manual work to find and match the same colony every visit. Detecting growth of roughly 10–15 mm per year requires errors of about a centimeter or less.

Obstacle 1 · Compute

Too many frames, too much work

Long underwater videos hold thousands of near-duplicate frames. Using all of them is slow, and rebuilding the model from scratch at every visit repeats work already done.

Obstacle 2 · Optics

Water can look like growth

Water clarity, light, and color change from visit to visit. A model that ignores this can read haze as a missing branch, or a shadow as new tissue.

Obstacle 3 · Trust

Identity and honesty

The system must know which object is “colony 12” on every visit and ignore fish, algae, and rock. A language model writing reports can also invent claims no measurement supports.

Can repeat underwater video of coral colonies be turned, efficiently and reliably, into a 4D model that measures how each colony changed, with honest error ranges and reports that never claim more than the data show?

02 / Approach

One persistent 4D model of the reef plot, not a new 3D model per dive.

Every visit shares one coordinate frame, water effects are corrected, and each visit updates the previous one, so what differs between visits is more likely biology than optics.

03 / Objectives

Three objectives, deliberately narrow in scope.

One reef site chosen with HBOI’s coral program, individually tagged colonies (preferably fast-growing branching corals), and one question: how did this colony’s surface area, volume, and branch count change between visits? We use frozen foundation models (SAM and a VLM) and compare every result with standard photogrammetry.

01Reconstruct

An efficient, water-aware 4D model of the reef plot

One persistent set of surface elements, each with a position, shape, and appearance at every visit. Elements are added where a colony grows and removed where it shrinks, so change is read directly from the model.

  • Choose frames. Keep frames that are sharp, clear, and add new viewpoints.
  • Fix a common frame. Permanent reference markers beside (not on) colonies, plus scale bars, tie every visit to one coordinate system.
  • Update, do not rebuild. Each visit starts from the previous state and re-optimizes mostly where things changed, using a surface-focused Gaussian method such as 2D Gaussian splatting.
  • Remove water effects. Each photo is modeled as the object’s true color dimmed and hazed by water:
I = J · e−βD·z + B∞ · (1 − e−βB·z)

I recorded color · J true color · z camera distance · βD, βB attenuation and backscatter · B∞ far-water color. Estimating J and the water jointly means a change in clarity is absorbed by the water terms, not mistaken for a change in the colony.

DeliverableVideo-to-4D pipeline with frames, time, and GPU-hours compared against full photogrammetry and per-visit rebuilding.
02Identify

Find and follow individual colonies

SAM outlines objects in keyframes and follows them through the video. A vision-language model then answers two questions for each outline: is this a coral colony? and is it the same colony as the tagged one from the last visit? using position relative to the markers, shape, and appearance.

Outlines from many views are lifted into 3D so each colony becomes its own piece of the model, with a stable identity across visits. Baseline: a 2D detection-and-tracking pipeline.

DeliverablePer-colony 3D models with identities, and tests of H2.
03Measure

Measure change, report honestly, and validate

  • Surface area & volume change from each colony’s closed 3D surface, reported as relative change.
  • Branch-count change from the colony skeleton, reported separately for branching colonies.
  • Growth/loss map showing where each surface point moved or disappeared.
  • Novel view synthesis so experts can inspect colonies from angles divers never filmed.
  • Verified reports. The generator may only restate numbers from the measurement table; a checker rejects any mismatch. Health statements appear only with validated support, otherwise the report says “cannot assess.”
DeliverableChange-measurement and reporting tool, CoralLapse-Bench, and tests of H3.
04 / Hypotheses

What we expect to show.

H1

A single water-aware 4D model, with all visits in one common frame and each visit initialized from the previous one, reduces false change from water and lighting and cuts computation versus per-visit reconstruction.

H2

SAM outlines checked by a VLM keep colony identity across visits more accurately than a 2D detection-and-tracking baseline.

H3

Video-derived area, volume and branch changes match phantoms and diver measurements within error comparable to photogrammetry, at a fraction of the effort, and reports contain no contradicted claim.

05 / Evaluation

CoralLapse-Bench

Validation does not depend on slow biological growth alone. Known-answer phantoms and same-day re-scans give ground truth at every visit.

Phantoms

3D-printed coral replicas of known surface area, volume, and branch count, in base and “grown” versions with known added volume, scanned on the reef like real colonies.

Test–retest scans

The same unchanged colonies and phantoms scanned twice on one day, which measures the noise floor: the smallest change distinguishable from error.

Real colonies

HBOI colonies with diver tape measurements of maximum extension at every visit, and conventional photogrammetry of the same data as a reference.

Questions and how we measure them

Are the right objects found and followed?
Overlap with expert outlines; correct identity rate across visits; non-coral objects wrongly included.
Is the shape accurate?
Area, volume, and branch-count error against phantoms; extension error against diver tape; agreement with photogrammetry.
Is the 4D model consistent over time?
Surface-point tracking error on phantoms with known growth; drift of unchanged regions; novel views vs. held-out frames.
How small a change can it detect?
Noise floor from test–retest scans; smallest known phantom growth detected.
Do water and light changes fool it?
Change error as turbidity and lighting differ, including controlled degradation, with and without water modeling and the common frame.
Are the reports honest?
Every number matches the measurements; expert rating of health statements; how often the system correctly says “cannot assess.”
Is it efficient?
Frames used, processing time, and GPU-hours against full photogrammetry.

08 / Impact

Why it matters, and what comes next.

Intellectual merit

  • Water-aware 4D model that separates true change from changes in water and lighting.
  • Colony identity tracking that joins segmentation with language understanding.
  • Change measurements with a measured noise floor, validated against known-size replicas.
  • Verified monitoring reports: trustworthy AI applied to science.

Broader impacts

Faster, cheaper, more repeatable growth measurement for coral restoration and monitoring in Florida. Code and CoralLapse-Bench released where agreements permit; graduate and undergraduate training at the interface of AI and marine science.

External funding path

  • Primary: NSF Division of Ocean Sciences (OTIC), scaling to more colonies, species, sites, and platforms.
  • Secondary: NSF CISE for trustworthy, verified language-grounded perception; NOAA coral reef conservation and restoration; ONR ocean sensing.
  • The seed work supplies a dataset and benchmark, a working prototype, quantitative results, and an established HBOI–CoECS team.
09 / Glossary

Key terms in plain language.

4D
3D plus time: one model of the same place whose shape and appearance at each visit are stored together, so each part of a colony can be followed.
Photogrammetry
Building a 3D model from many overlapping photos. The standard tool for measuring coral growth.
3D Gaussian Splatting
Building a 3D scene from millions of small, soft, colored blobs that can be drawn quickly from any viewpoint.
SAM
“Segment Anything,” an AI that outlines objects in images and can follow them through video.
Vision-language model (VLM)
An AI trained on images and text together; here it checks whether an outline is a coral and whether it is the same colony as before.
Phantom
A 3D-printed coral replica of known size and volume, used to test measurement accuracy.
Noise floor
The smallest change the system can tell apart from measurement error, measured by scanning an unchanged object twice.
